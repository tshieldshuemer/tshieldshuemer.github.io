## Overview

This page covers my section of an 84 page group report, completed as part of a 3rd year civil engineering group project, in which we were tasked to choose a site within Dublin city to develop and design a multi-purpose arena. The brief required the arena to host at least one olympic ice rink, with a minimum seated capacity of 5,000 or a standing capacity of 8,000. 

My role within the team of 5 was to design the stadium itself, covering architectural layout and CAD design using both Revit & Solidworks. I was responsible for all drawings & renders in the report including plans, elevations & sections.

I also coordinated with team members regarding design choices that had impacts on traffic assessment, fire safety, univeral design, U-values, energy efficiency and material sustainability.

### 7.2. Rink Design 

The main rink was designed in line with the IIHF manual. A size of 60 m x 30 m with an 8.5 m corner fillet was selected - the largest official dimensions. This was done to avoid controversy over a narrower play zone (as was seen at the Milan–Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics), while a wider arc radius also allows for easier resurfacing.
The rink itself was modelled in Solidworks to allow for more precise dimensioning and control and was then imported into Revit through a IFC linking.

### 7.3. Bowl Design 

At a seated capacity over 5000, it was decided to split the seating into an upper and lower concourse, allowing for steeper seat tiering while maintaining adequate C-Values, while simultaneously allowing for a circulation gangway at the top of the first tier.

### 7.4. Rake Angle & Sight Lines

The stadium bowl was designed in compliance with the SGSA’s Guide to Safety at Sports Grounds, which is the main design guidance document for stadia in the UK.
The SGSA guide sets a rake angle of 34° for seated areas, 25° for standing.
Alongside rake, the bowl design considered C-values, the vertical clearance between a spectator's sight line and the eye of the person in front of them. 
A minimum C-Value of 90 mm was decided upon, in line with SGSA guidance, to maintain a good view for spectators, while keeping the rake angle and overall bowl height within regulation.
An eye height of 1.2m above the tread was used in C-Value calculations, taken from 0.5m behind the row front edge of each tier, with the focal point set at the top of the nearside kickboards.

### 7.5. Ground Floor 

<img src="/GF Plan R5.png" alt="Ground Floor" width="1200" class="lightbox-trigger border-4 border-black cursor-pointer hover:opacity-90 transition"/>

The ground floor of the arena houses both the professional and the public ice rinks, (both full sized 60 x 30 m). The main arena entrance and lobby is located on the south side of the plan, with three sets of turnstiles to control entry for events. Revolving turnstiles to the right of the lobby allow for one way exit.

<img src="/DCS00-CVE-ZZ-00-DR-A-0003-Main Entrance.png" alt="Main Entrance Detail" width="1200" class="lightbox-trigger border-4 border-black cursor-pointer hover:opacity-90 transition"/>

Revolving doors to the left control entry to the public rink, and limit air exchange/heat transfer between the colder rink spaces and the lobby area as the doors are never fully open as in a swing door.
A secondary entrance on the north-east of the plan is used solely for event entry, with both entrances having a WC block. 

<img src="/DCS00-CVE-ZZ-00-DR-A-0004-GF WC Detail.png" alt="WC Detail" width="1200" class="lightbox-trigger border-4 border-black cursor-pointer hover:opacity-90 transition"/>

The north and north-west sections of the ground floor are separated from the public and house team entry, locker rooms & facilities as well as resurfacer/zamboni storage & maintenance. As per the IIHF manual, team facilities are identical for both sides, with a central hallway between the two leading to the player tunnel/main rink.

<img src="/DCS00-CVE-ZZ-00-DR-A-0002-Team Area & Zambonis.png" alt="Team Area & Zambonis" width="1200" class="lightbox-trigger border-4 border-black cursor-pointer hover:opacity-90 transition"/>

The resurfacer room is located between the two rinks, allowing for shared use. It houses the melt pit as well as two Zambonis. This design was decided upon as it allows for redundancy in case of a resurfacer breakdown and a sharing of utilities.
The refrigeration plant is housed within the space under the first tier of the seating bowl.

### 7.6. Stage

The arena bowl features an end stage to the east, with a plan of circa 10 x 30 m. 
A backstage area acts as back of house for the arena, with large roller doors and ramps to accommodate delivery of large equipment and removal of waste. It is also the storage area for arena equipment such as removable seating or the mats used to cover the ice for ‘in the round’ events.
The stage is designed to be convertible, with a telescopic seating stand along its back wall. 
The telescopic stands have a capacity of 192 seats, while folding chairs placed on the stage add an additional 260 potential seats, though at flat elevation the view may be poor for back rows.

### 7.7. Circulation

Movement between floors is primarily facilitated by two circulation cores, one on the north side and one on the south side of the arena. Each core features 8 high capacity KONE Transys DX lifts, as well as two stairwells. Additional staircases facilitate movement on the east and west sides.
